### Slicers

Every data page has the same two slicers:

| Slicer | Field | Hierarchy / values | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signup date | `vectra_analytics fact_subscriptions[signup_date]` | Date hierarchy: Year → Quarter → Month → Day; Year is the expanded level | Filters fact rows by **signup** date, not by revenue month or cancellation date |
| Plan type | `vectra_analytics fact_subscriptions[plan_type]` | Enterprise, Growth, Starter, Unassigned | Filters the fact side; because the relationship is single-direction, it does not filter `dim_customers` |

> [!warning] The date slicer is not a reporting-period control
> The hierarchy is built on `signup_date`. Changing it filters which customers signed up in that period and their later rows; it does not select a monthly revenue period. The report has no standalone date table and no month-end snapshot, so the slicer cannot answer “what was MRR in March?” It answers “which rows belong to customers who signed up in the selected signup period?”

### Relationship consequence

The model has one active, single-direction relationship:

```text
dim_customers[customer_id] (1) ────< fact_subscriptions[customer_id] (*)
```

- Filtering `dim_customers[region]` or `[acquisition_channel]` filters the fact.
- Filtering a fact field such as `plan_type` or `signup_date` does not filter the customer dimension.
- Measures that divide by `DISTINCTCOUNT(dim_customers[customer_id])` therefore keep a 1,200-customer denominator when a fact-side slicer is applied. This is why the `Annual ARPU` chart is not plan-level ARPU. See [[08 - DAX and KPI Dictionary]].

### What is filtered by default

No default slicer selections were found. The cards and charts therefore show the full imported model when the page opens, except where a visual has its own filter. Two tables have a visual-level filter:

```text
status_cleaned IN ("Churned", "Unassigned")
```

That filter applies to:

- The Customer page watchlist table.
- The Products page summary table.

It does **not** apply to the cards or charts on those pages. This produces a sharp difference between the headline cards and the table below them.

### What the cards actually show

All values below are unfiltered `MODEL-COMPUTED` values, not rendered card text.

| Card | Value | What it measures | Why the label overstates it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `Total MRR` | $39,146,742 | Sum of every non-null `mrr_amount` row | Includes Active, Cancelled, Churned, Downgraded, and null-status rows; not demonstrated current MRR |
| `Enterprise Revenue` | $14,147,984 | Sum of MRR where `plan_type = "Enterprise"` | 36.14% of the all-row total; the largest plan, not a majority |
| `Customers Enterprise %` | 36.14% | Enterprise revenue ÷ total revenue | Revenue share, not customer share; returns 100% if the page is filtered to Enterprise |
| `Annual ARPU` | $32,622.29 | All-row MRR ÷ 1,200 distinct dimension customers | Not annualised; numerator spans every row, denominator is all customers |
| `Unassigned Revenue` | $4,728,753 | MRR where `plan_type = "Unassigned"` | Revenue in the total whose plan attribute is unmapped; not a separate stream |

> [!warning] “Annual ARPU” is the most misleading card on the page
> There is no annual period in the calculation. It is closer to an **all-observed-row revenue per customer** figure. The label implies a yearly per-user yield that the model does not calculate. See [[08 - DAX and KPI Dictionary#6. Annual ARPU]].

### The trend chart

`MRR Growth Trend (2021 - 2025)` is a stacked area chart of `Total MRR` by the `signup_date` date hierarchy. The year level is active, with quarter/month/day available for drill-down.

It is **not**:

- Monthly recurring revenue by revenue month.
- A month-over-month or year-over-year growth chart.
- A net revenue retention chart.
- A current-MRR trend.

It is a sum of all transaction rows grouped by the year in which the customer signed up. Because each customer has many rows and can have several signup dates, the visual mixes cohort timing with all-row revenue. Its shape should not be read as a revenue time series.

### Plan charts

**Monetization Yield by Account Tier ($).** The category is `plan_type`, not `dim_customers[tier]`. The measure is `Annual ARPU`, which divides each plan’s revenue by all 1,200 customers because the plan filter does not flow to the dimension. As modelled:

| Plan | Plan revenue | `Annual ARPU` as displayed (÷ 1,200) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | $14,147,984 | $11,789.99 |
| Growth | $10,106,914 | $8,422.43 |
| Starter | $10,163,091 | $8,469.24 |
| Unassigned | $4,728,753 | $3,940.63 |

These are **not** plan-level ARPU values. A plan-specific denominator would produce different numbers. The chart title’s “Account Tier” also implies the `tier` field, which is not used.

**Churn Rate (%) by Plan Tier.** The measure returns Cancelled rows ÷ all rows in the current filter context. Unfiltered, the bars are:

| Plan | Cancelled rows | All rows | `Churn Rate %` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | 601 | 3,656 | 16.44% |
| Growth | 452 | 2,493 | 18.13% |
| Starter | 424 | 2,619 | 16.19% |
| Unassigned | 215 | 1,232 | 17.45% |

This is a row share, not customer churn. It excludes 1,565 rows with raw `status = "Churned"`. If both Cancelled and Churned are treated as churn, the plan rates are 32.17%, 32.49%, 31.73%, and 35.71% respectively — much more similar than the chart’s label suggests.

**Customer Volume by Plan Tier.** The bar uses `COUNT(customer_id)`, so it plots 3,656, 2,493, 2,619, and 1,232 rows. It is not 1,200 distinct customers. 576 customers have rows in all four plan categories, so plan “volume” is subscription-row volume, not a customer segment count.

### Overview table

The table has no visual title and no filter. It shows four rows:

| Plan Tier | MRR ($) | Share (%) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | $14,147,984 | 36.14% |
| Growth | $10,106,914 | 25.82% |
| Starter | $10,163,091 | 25.96% |
| Unassigned | $4,728,753 | 12.08% |

The share calculation is arithmetically valid as a share of all-row MRR. It is not a share of current MRR, customers, or units.

### What the page answers — and does not

| It can support | It cannot support |
|---|---|
| A descriptive split of the imported all-row MRR by plan | A current-MRR or financial statement figure |
| The fact that Enterprise is the largest plan at 36.14% | A claim that Enterprise is a majority |
| A rough comparison of Cancelled-row shares by plan | A customer churn rate or a causal churn explanation |
| A count of subscription rows by plan | A distinct customer count by plan |
| The size of the Unassigned plan bucket | A claim that $4.73M is a separate revenue stream |

| Filtered population | Rows | Distinct customers | MRR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

This is a report label defect and should be corrected before the table is shown to a business audience.

## Page 4 — Insights (`STRATEGIC INSIGHTS & RECOMMENDATIONS`)

### Purpose

A static narrative synthesis with three insight themes and three proposed initiatives. It contains no charts, no KPI cards, no slicers, and no data-bound text.

### Visual inventory

| Visual | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Image | Full-width banner: `STRATEGIC INSIGHTS & RECOMMENDATIONS` |
| 2 | Text box | `STRATEGIC INSIGHTS & RECOMMENDATIONS`; three themes |
| 3 | Text box | `STRATEGIC ACTION PLAN`; three initiatives |
| 4–7 | Action buttons | Overview, Customer, Products, Insights navigation |
| 8 | Shape | Left navigation background |

The text boxes use static paragraphs. Bracketed strings such as `[Total MRR]`, `[Churned Customers]`, and `[Average ARPU]` are literal copy in the text runs, not bound measure values. The page therefore does not dynamically update its numbers when the model changes.

### The three insight themes

1. **Revenue Concentration Risk** — says Enterprise contributes the majority share, that high concentration creates severe volatility, and that executive oversight and CSM mapping are required for top-tier clients.
2. **Churn Vulnerability in Mid-Tier Plans** — says cancellation counts are disproportionately clustered in Growth and Starter, points to onboarding friction or feature drop-off, and proposes automated health triggers when mid-tier usage falls below thresholds.
3. **Data Pipeline Governance & Attribution Gaps** — says Unassigned plan categories distort attribution and proposes rigid validation during ELT.

### The three proposed initiatives

1. **Retention & Anti-Churn Engine (Customer Success)** — target mid-tier accounts; deploy CRM alerts when active sessions fall by 20% or more; expected impact: 15% reduction in voluntary churn over two quarters.
2. **Feature Tiering & Value Gap Alignment (Product Strategy)** — re-evaluate feature access between Growth and Enterprise; use migration incentives; expected impact: increase in overall ARPU.
3. **Staging Pipeline Sanity & Governance (Data Operations)** — map blank strings to an explicit Unassigned flag and enforce foreign keys; expected impact: 100% data integrity for board-level reporting.

### What the page gets right

- It recognises that data quality is part of the business story.
- It separates diagnosis from proposed action.
- It identifies plan attribution as a real governance gap.
- It frames retention as a cross-functional Customer Success problem, not only a reporting problem.

### What the page does not support

| Claim on the page | What the model shows | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise contributes the **majority** share | Enterprise is 36.14% of all-row MRR; it is the largest plan, not a majority | **Contradicted** |
| Losing a small percentage of Enterprise accounts creates severe volatility | The top ten customers account for about 2.2% of all-row MRR; customer-level concentration is not demonstrated | **Not supported** |
| Cancellations are disproportionately clustered in Growth and Starter | Cancelled counts: Enterprise 601, Growth 452, Starter 424, Unassigned 215. Rates: Growth 18.13%, Enterprise 16.44%, Starter 16.19% | **Not supported as written** |
| A 20% session decline is a usable alert threshold | No session/telemetry data exists in the model; the threshold is a management hypothesis | **Unverified** |
| The intervention will reduce voluntary churn by 15% | No experiment, control group, baseline, or model supports the figure | **Unsupported** |
| The governance work will produce 100% data integrity | No validation results or acceptance criteria are supplied | **Unsupported** |

> [!danger] Do not present the Insights page as an analytical result

## What a reader should do first

1. **Do not quote a card without reading its measure definition.** Start with [[08 - DAX and KPI Dictionary]].
2. **Treat every count as a row count until proven otherwise.** The fact has 10,000 rows and 1,200 customers.
3. **Treat `Total MRR` as an all-row sum, not current MRR.** The fact grain is not established.
4. **Check the table filter before reading a churn percentage.** The Products table changes the denominator to 4,930.
5. **Do not use the Insights page as evidence.** Use [[10 - Business Findings]] for what the model supports.
6. **Open the PBIX in Power BI Desktop if a rendered view is required.** This vault describes the definition, not screenshots.
